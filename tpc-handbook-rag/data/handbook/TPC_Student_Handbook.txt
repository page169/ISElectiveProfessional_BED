PREFACE
This handbook will highlight not only the academic 
policies of the school but also the history, vision, mission, 
goals, objectives, philosophy, the facilities and services and 
all other relevant information that parents and students
need to know about Talibon Polytechnic College.
Talibon Polytechnic College greets you and your 
children. The administration, teachers and staff believe that 
your child has gained enough learnings from different 
sources. You, as parents have nurtured and guided them 
well as part of their advancement and growth. We, the 
second parents in school are so grateful to you for 
entrusting your children to TPC. We always do our best to 
support in the formation of their inner selves as instrument 
for local and national development.
Let us collaborate to put into action the vision, mission 
of the school. You can come to the school anytime you wish 
and share your ideas and noble thoughts to usher our 
students towards a very productive and memorable 
education experience.
This handbook has been prepared to acquaint you with the 
policies of the school. We encourage you to read this
handbook thoroughly so that violations can be avoided. 
Hopefully, Talibon Polytechnic College can produce citizen 
worthy to replicate the deeds of our heroes; thus, become 
the agents of change and make a difference.

GENERAL PROVISIONS
Chapter 1
PRELIMINARY MATTERS
Section 1. This document shall be called and known as the 
“Talibon Polytechnic College Student Manual.”
Section 2. Coverage – This Manual shall apply to and 
govern all bonafide students in all level and curricula of 
Talibon Polytechnic College.
Section 3. Purpose – This Manual is intended primarily to 
serve as a guide to student conduct and matters affecting 
student life.
Section 4. Interpretation – All provisions of this Manual 
shall, in case of conflict or ambiguity, be interpreted in favor 
of the students.
Section 5. Definitions of Terms – The terms as used in this 
Student Manual are defined as follows:
TPC refers to the Talibon Polytechnic College.
President refers to the highest official of TPC
Director of Student Affairs and Services refers to 
the officer designated to oversee the operation of the 
different units in-charge of student personnel 
services in TPC.
Recognition/Accreditation means the authority to 
operate at TPC granted to student organizations by 
authority of the President.

ARTICLE II

Chapter 1
ADMISSION POLICIES
Section 1. Graduates of recognized private and public high 
school may be admitted to Talibon Polytechnic College 
without any regard of age, gender, religion or race.
Section 2. The Committee on Admission of the college is 
tasked to screen the credentials of the applicants.
Section 3. Talibon Polytechnic College reserves the right to 
refuse any applicant whose qualifications do not meet the 
standards of the school.
Chapter 2
ADMISSION TEST
Section 1. All applicants for admission to Talibon 
Polytechnic College for all the degree programs shall take 
the TPC Admission Test.
Section 2. The TPC Admission Test is a standardized test 
requiring careful attention to instructions and time limits. 
It is designed to provide convenient, objectively-scorable 
measure of general cognitive ability or intelligence in the 
form of a profile containing five verbal (Information, 
Comprehension, Arithmetic, Similarities and Vocabulary) 
and five performances (Digital Symbol, Picture Completion, 
Spatial, Picture Arrangement and Object Assembly) subtest 
scores.

Chapter 5
ACADEMIC INFORMATION 
A. Academic Load
Section 1. The study load of a regular student is the 
number of units prescribed by the curriculum in which the 
student is enrolled.
Section 2. A student is considered full time or carrying a 
full load when he carries all subjects prescribed in his 
curriculum for a particular school semester.
Section 3. Students should follow the study load 
prescribed by his/her curricula or as assigned and 
approved by his/her respective Deans and Registrar.
Section 4. During Summer Term, a student may register 
only in nine (9) units, except for graduating students who 
shall be allowed a higher load not exceeding 12 units.

TPC STUDENT HANDBOOK v.04

Section 5. Only graduating students are allowed to 
overload, subject to their academic standing.
Section 6. A graduating student may be permitted to carry 
a heavier load but not to exceed 27 units subject upon to 
the approval by authorities concerned.
Section 7. Students who are scholastic delinquents are not 
allowed to have an overload.
Section 8. Teacher Education students taking up practice 
teaching are allowed to overload or have other subjects 
taken alongside with practice teaching not more than six (6) 
units.
Section 9. Load of transfer students shall be limited only 
to those subjects without prerequisite, until such time this 
subject had already been validated/accredited.
Section 10. The Dean/Program Head may limit the 
academic load of student who are employed outside the 
institution whether full time or part time. (MORPHE 
guidelines, specifically Article XIX, Section 92, allow for a 
maximum overload of six units for graduating students, as

internship period and/or according to the Institution’s 
policy.)

Section 1. In addition to Section 9 of the Education Act of

information, and the Civil Code of the Philippines, which 
protects privacy rights. The Family Code of the Philippines 
also reinforces privacy, especially when students reach 
legal age. These laws collectively guarantee students' rights 
to access and control their school records
Section 2. A student of legal age (18 or older) in the 
Philippines can deny their parents access to their school 
records. Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act 
No. 10173), individuals have control over their personal 
data, and schools must obtain the student's consent to 
disclose information to third parties, including parents, 
unless required by law. Once a student reaches legal age, 
they are the sole authority over their academic records, 
unless there is a legal basis for disclosure.

POLICY FOR PERSONS WITH DISABILITY 
(PWD) AND SOLO PARENTS 
Section 1. Purpose. The aim of this policy is to provide 
Persons with Disabilities (PWD) and Solo Parents with equal 
access and opportunity for admission, enrollment, and 
academic achievement at Talibon Polytechnic College. TPC 
endeavors to provide a diverse, inclusive, and caring 
learning environment for all its students.

TPC STUDENT HANDBOOK v.04
49
Section 2. Scope. This policy shall be observed by all 
potential students applying for admission, and by those 
requesting enrollment or re-enrollment in degree and nondegree programs of Talibon Polytechnic College, whether or 
not they have disabilities or are solo parents.
Section 3. Policy Statement
1. Non-Discrimination. Talibon Polytechnic College
adheres to the values of equal opportunity, nondiscrimination, and respect for diversity. The college
believes in extending fair access to education to
persons with disabilities and solo parents.
2. Persons with Disabilities (PWD). The college has a
policy of reasonable accommodation for the PWDs
as per the laws. The institution accepts that the
PWDs have special difficulties and offers extra
assistance to enable them to become fully involved
in college life.
Section 4. Admission Requirements. PWD applicants will 
be judged on academic criteria only. The college will not 
apply extra requirements that are discriminatory and 
inaccessible to the PWDs.
Section 5. Reasonable Accommodations. The college will 
also make changes to facilities, services, and policy where 
appropriate. This might include, but is not limited to:
1. Accessible campus facilities (ramps, elevators,
designated seating).
a. Altered examination procedures (extra time,
quiet room, screen readers).
b. Supply of assistive technologies.

TPC STUDENT HANDBOOK v.04
50
Section 6. Disclosure and Confidentiality. PWD applicants 
are encouraged to reveal their disability status at the time 
of admission for the school to know the possible programs 
specific for their needs. All personal data pertaining to the 
disability will be treated confidentially in accordance with
applicable data protection legislation.
Section 7. Collecting the disability information about the 
student’s disability will solely be used for providing 
accommodations and support. This information will not be 
used for any forms of discrimination or deny opportunities.
Section 8. Solo Parents. Solo parents, as classified by the 
Solo Parents' Welfare Act (Republic Act No. 8972), are 
entitled to assistance during the admission process and 
through their entire academic life. The school acknowledges 
the balancing act between study and parental duties and 
strives to alleviate the solo parent students' burdens.
1. Admission Consideration: Solo parents who qualify
for admission to the chosen academic program will
be considered without prejudice. Priority
scholarship or financial assistance consideration
may be given based on single parent status,
especially for those financially underprivileged.
2. Support for Solo Parents: Reasonable support
measures will be made available by the college to
help solo parents meet their academic
requirements, such as:
a. Flexible class or examination schedules where
feasible.
b. On-campus childcare access or help.
c. Counseling and academic as well as emotional
counseling services.

ARTICLE V
ADMINISTRATIVE DUE PROCESS
Section 1. An administrative due process refers to the 
procedural fairness that schools must provide to students 
before imposing formative intervention like suspensions or 
expulsions. It ensures students are treated fairly and have 
a chance to respond to accusations. Key elements include 
proper notice of charges, the right to a hearing, and a 
decision based on evidence. 
Section 2. Disciplinary matters are those acts of students 
referred to in the College Student Manual that need 
formative intervention through Warning, Reprimand,
Suspension, Exclusion or Expulsion. It is conducted if there 
is a complaint, letter, report or memo pertaining to an 
alleged commission of an act by any student of the College
that may be considered as a misconduct, offense, infraction 
or violation of a policy, rule or regulation of the College.
Section 3. The procedure is aimed at determining whether 
a student, who is under investigation for the commission of 
an alleged misconduct, offense, infraction or violation of
any of the college policies, rules or regulations, is liable or 
not; and if so, the proper formative intervention shall be 
meted, but if not, the case shall be dismissed.

ARTICLE VIII
EDUCATION TOUR/FIELD POLICIES AND 
GUIDELINE
The primary objective of the school field trips is to provide 
an educational experience in keeping with planned 
curriculum, while exposing students to new cultural and 
physical opportunities not found in daily life. Moreover, 
field trips are designed to encourage group participation 
and bonding among the students and teachers. With those 
objectives in mind, the following guidelines have been 
designed to help students, teacher, chaperones, and 
parents get the most of their field trip experience.
A. Student’s Roles
Section 1. All students are expected to participate in field 
trips but not forcibly imposed.
Section 2. Because field trips are an opportunity for 
students to stretch themselves in new and unique ways, 
Students should show a positive attitude when facing 
unfamiliar situations or challenges.
Section 3. Throughout the field trip, students are expected 
to uphold regular school rules and show respect for each 
other, teacher and other adult supervisor.
Section 4. During a field trip, students are representative 
of our institution, so their behavior should reflect that at 
all times.

TPC STUDENT HANDBOOK v.04
138
Section 5. Field trips begin at the designated meeting place 
(usually the town bus or ferry terminal) if a student requires 
alternative arrival, departure, or other transportation 
arrangements, parents must notify teacher in writing prior 
to the trip.
B. Teacher’s Roles
Section 1. Teachers are to design field trip experiences that 
appropriately dovetail with the existing curriculum, provide 
unique experiences are age appropriate for the students 
participating, and are of reasonable cost.
Section 2. Teachers shall determine the number of 
chaperones needed for a field trip.
Section 3. Teachers shall coordinate with the
transportation to and from the event, assess costs, assign 
chaperone duties, and make other logistical arrangements 
well in advance of the field trip.
Section 4. Teachers shall then communicate those plans 
to the College President for Board of Trustees’ approval.
Section 5. Once a field trip has been organized and 
approved, teacher shall be responsible for fund raising and 
communicating logistical plans to parents and obtaining 
permission slips from them.
C. Personnel In-Charge
Section 1. The role of the Personnel In-Charge (PIC) is to 
support teachers and to provide additional supervision for 
the entire group of students, as needed. They may be called 
on to perform a variety of roles, depending on the nature of 
the trip.

of the event and should provide a positive example of the 
students.

small group of students, as directed by the teachers.
Section 4. Prior to and throughout the trip, PIC should be 
in communication with teachers to determine specific roles 
and responsibilities.
Section 5. PIC help teachers monitor student behavior and 
refer any issues to teachers.
Section 6. Students are under the direct supervision of the 
teacher in-charge at all times.
Section 7. During overnight field trips, PIC is responsible 
for student supervision 24 hours a day, unless otherwise 
determined by teachers, and should remain with the group 
throughout the entire field trips.
Section 8. PIC may not bring non-students on the trip.
Section 9. PIC should not smoke in the presence of 
students or any enclosed areas.
Section 10. PIC shall not drink alcohol or use illegal drugs

Section 11. PIC, who drive their personal vehicles, carrying

ARTICLE I
PURPOSE
Section 1. As per CHED Memorandum 63, Series of 2017, 
on “Policies and Guidelines on Local Off-Campus Activities, 
on “Implementing Guidelines on the Conduct of OffCampus Activities, the Talibon Polytechnic College (TPC) 
created Policy and Guidelines, in compliance with the 
requirements of the Commission on Higher Education. 
These policies and guidelines serve as the basis for the 
conduct of all local off-campus activities for all students of 
the College. The content is an adaptation of the abovementioned CHED Memorandum and polish according to 
the needs of the students and the College. The inclusion of 
all departments in this policy specifies its importance and 
the desire of the College to unify and protect all students 
from any unnecessary incident in the conduct of all local 
Off-Campus activities. “In the Philippines, Higher 
Education Institutions (HEIs) ensure sustainable teaching 
and learning delivery process through the conduct of offcampus activities. These are activities conducted by HEIs 
to supplement and facilitate a more meaningful learning 
experience for students in addition to the regular 
instructional programs that are in accordance with specific 
degree program requirements. (Article I, CMO 63, s. 2017)

ARTICLE II
DEFINITION OF TERMS
Section 1. For purposes of clarification, the following terms 
are duly defined as stipulated in Section 7, Article V, CMO 
63, s. 2017.
1. Approved curriculum - “refers to the curriculum
duly approved by the HEI and duly noted by the
CHED regional offices (CHEDROs).” (Section 7.1,
Article V, CMO 63, s. 2017)
2. Curricular activities – “are required off- campus
activities and are an integral part of the
instructional program. All students are expected to
attend the scheduled off-campus activity since it is
part of the regularly scheduled class time.” (Section
7.2, Article V, CMO 63, s. 2017)
A. Educational Tour – “refer to off- campus 
learning activities involving mobility of students 
with the supervision of authorized personnel 
outside the premises of the institution which 
lasts for more than one (1) day, and involves
relatively more places of destination than a field 
trip in accordance with specific degree program 
requirements.” (Section 7.2a, Article V, CMO 63, 
s. 2017)
B. Field Trip – “refer to off-campus learning 
activities involving mobility of students with the 
supervision of authorized personnel outside the 
premises of the institution but is of relatively 
shorter duration usually lasting for only one (1) 
day and with fewer places of destination.” 
(Section 7.2b, Article V, CMO 63, s. 2017)

Section 1. The objectives of this policy underline the basis

the approved curriculum;” (Section 5.1, Article III,

activities to those students who will be participating

GENERAL RULES
Section 1. The conduct of all off-campus activities must be 
designed, determined and approved in accordance with the 
curriculum requirement and its relevance therein.

CMO 63, s.2017 is a must for every student-participant of
any Local Off-Campus activity. All requirements shall be 
submitted to the Office of the Student Affairs and Services 
two (2) weeks or 14 days before the conduct of the activity. 
The Activity Permit shall only be issued upon compliance of 
“before the activity” needed documents. The rest of the 
requirements shall be submitted immediately after the 
activity for compliance with CHED. “Failure to do so shall 
be a cause for imposition of the sanctions as provided in 
the CMO without prejudice to other liabilities under

three (3) days before the conduct of the off-campus
activity.

A. Before the Off-Campus Activity
1. Course Requirement
a. Course Syllabus and Comprehensive
Semestral Report– “reflects the relevance of
requiring an educational tour and field trip.”
(Section 11.2, a.1, Article VII, CMO 63, s.
2017). “The destination and schedule should
be relevant to the subject matter.” (Section
11.2, a.1, Article VII, CMO 63, s. 2017) In
order to minimize cost, destinations for local
off-campus activities should be conducted in
nearby places.
b. Certificate of Compliance – “duly notarized,
certified correct by the personnel-in-charge
(PIC), recommending approval by the VicePresident for Academic Affairs (VPAA) and
duly approved by the President or Head of
the HEI or his/her authorized
representative.” (Annex A, CMO 63, s. 2017)
Must be submitted to the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) two (2) weeks or
fourteen (14) days prior to the conduct of the
activity.
c. Report of Compliance – must be submitted
to the Office of the Student Affairs and
Services together with the rest of the
requirements two (2) weeks or fourteen (14)
days prior to the conduct of the local offcampus activity.

2. Destination: Itinerary of the trip – the Itinerary
of the local off-campus activity must be clearly
stated. Side trips are prohibited and strictly
discouraged.
3. Students’ Handbook/College Policy – The
guidelines and requirements for the conduct of
local off-campus activities shall be included in
the Students’ Handbook/College Policy.
4. Student Documents
a. Parental/Guardian’s Consent – This
document shall be duly signed by
parent/guardian. Please be reminded that
only students with signed
Parental/Guardian’s consent are allowed to
join the activity. This is for strict compliance.
b. Medical Clearance – This document shall be
obtained from the College, Municipal Health
Unit or any government Physician.
5. Certificate of Appointment for Personnel-inCharge (PIC): As stated in Article IV, Section 6 of
this policy, the Personnel-in-charge shall be
appointed for specific local off-campus activities
with appropriate documents and training.
6. Duly approved Breakdown of Fund/Fees: “The
fees to be collected from the students must be
duly approved and disseminated to concerned
stakeholders… there should be a breakdown of
fund sources and other resources properly
secured and accounted for.” (Section 11.2, a.1,
Article VII, CMO 63, s. 2017)

7. LGU/NGO: Endorsement from the Local
Government Unit/Non-Governmental
Organization of the venue of the local offcampus activity is necessary to ensure the safety

B. During the Off-Campus Activity
1. Attendance and Safety
a. List of participants must be checked by the
Personnel-in-charge at the beginning,
during and after the conduct of the offcampus activity.
b. He/she also must guarantee that the
seating capacity of each vehicle be
respected.
c. No student-participant shall be allowed to
ride on the roof or on the boarding platform
of the vehicle.
d. Activities/program must be properly 
followed/adjusted if the need arises.
C. After the Off-Campus activity
1. Learning Journals - Students are required to
write a learning journal at the end of the local
off- campus activity.
2. Assessment Report/Evaluation Report –
Faculty Assessment Report regarding the
activity.
3. Expenditure Report – Breakdown of Expenses
